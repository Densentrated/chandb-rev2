Measured broadband

What the tests actually say

Every census tract in Suffolk County, Massachusetts and the nine-county San Francisco Bay Area, scored by the speed tests run inside it — and, just as importantly, by how much evidence that score rests on.

Read this before the numbers

A measured speed is not an availability map. Ookla records what a person got on the plan they already pay for, on the device they used. A tract with no fibre at all can post fast numbers on good cable, and a tract full of gigabit fibre can look slow if the people testing it buy the cheapest tier.

No tests means unmeasured, never unserved. Tracts with no speed test are shown as missing data throughout this page — a dashed outline and the words “no test” — and are never drawn as zero, blank or slow.

A number resting on 12 tests is not the number resting on 2,000. Every figure here carries its evidence tier, thin-evidence tracts are hatched and dimmed rather than listed unqualified, and the evidence chart below exists purely to show how far a sparsely-tested tract can wander.

The FCC’s claimed-coverage layer — the self-reported map federal funding is allocated from — and the Census subscription figures are not published in this lake yet. Until they are, nothing here can tell you whether a provider’s claim is true.
